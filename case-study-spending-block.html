01 / Find a useful place to start

Start with the people.
Then decide what to build.

We started with no defined solution. I ran lightning talks and a workshop with vulnerability specialists, designers and researchers.

We turned the evidence into opportunities, then chose what we could act on.

The design question

How could we help people interrupt unwanted spending, while keeping the choice in their hands?

02 / Give customers the choice

Their boundary.
Their decision.

Customers chose which merchants to block. We never said a purchase was wrong.

Sharing a reason was optional.

03 / A pause in their own words

A reminder from
present you to future you.

During setup, customers could write themselves a reminder. If they tried to unblock the merchant later, we showed them their own words.

The product creates a moment to reconsider. The customer stays in control.

The delivery decision

A useful first step.
Honest about its limits.

With one engineer and two weeks, we chose a first release with clear limits.

Specific merchants, not whole categories

Inconsistent merchant categorisation ruled out reliable category-wide blocks.

Card payments, not every way to pay

Bank-transfer restrictions needed support-team intervention.

A reminder, without a cooldown

Time-based blocks and a delay before unblocking were outside the first release.

Let’s make products feel clear and useful.
