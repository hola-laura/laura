01 / Overview

After the previous pillar fixed engagement — getting more customers to check their recommendations — a new problem surfaced. Customers were opening the recommendation, reading it, and then leaving to sense-check on another broker. I led the design of the decision moment: the two screens between "here's a recommendation" and "I'm going to commit."

02 / The problem

We help users get a recommendation, but not make a decision.

Users were dropping off at the point of highest intent because we didn't effectively support decision-making in-app. At the moment of receiving a recommendation, three things were going wrong at once:

  • Trust. They didn't have enough reassurance to commit.
  • Timing. They weren't sure whether to act now or wait.
  • Continuity. They lost the thread in the handoff from Monzo to web.

As a result, many customers used the journey as a sense-check — looking at our recommendation and then defaulting back to other brokers they already knew. This is a decision, timing, and trust problem under uncertainty — not a discovery problem.

03 / Discovery

We shadowed customers through real decisions — not imagined ones.

Research ran a three-part study: moderated walk-throughs with 18 customers mid-decision, a clickstream analysis of 12 weeks of recommendation journeys, and unmoderated trust-diary tasks where customers logged what made them hesitate.

  • 0%left the flow to sense-check with another broker before committing
  • 0 daysaverage gap between receiving a recommendation and acting on it
  • 0%dropped off at the Monzo→web handoff screen
  • 0live decision-moment interviews with real customers
04 / Principles

We built the whole project around three words: trust, timing, continuity.

  1. 01

    Trust — show the working.

    The customer should be able to see why this is their recommendation, not just what it is. No magic. No black box.

  2. 02

    Timing — answer "now or later?" before they ask.

    If the offer expires, say so. If nothing changes by waiting, say that too. Timing should feel like a decision, not a guess.

  3. 03

    Continuity — no lost threads at the handoff.

    The jump from Monzo to the broker's web journey is where intent evaporates. Carry state, tone, and confidence across the seam.

05 / Process

From a problem statement on the wall to a shipped decision moment.

01

Frame the real problem

Before pixels, we spent a week rewriting the brief. We replaced "improve the recommendation screen" with "support a decision under uncertainty." That reframing changed everything downstream.

02

Three prototypes, three stances

One prototype led with reassurance, one led with urgency, one led with comparison. Each argued a different theory about why customers hesitate.

03

Test with customers mid-decision

We recruited customers actively comparing brokers that week and handed them prototypes. Watching real decision-making is very different from watching a usability test.

04

Ship the handoff fix first

The biggest drop-off was the Monzo→web handoff. We fixed that as a standalone release before the full decision-moment redesign, so we could measure the lift in isolation.

07 / UI explorations

Four headers we explored before committing.

I led a round of header explorations to answer a question the whole team had: how much room should the broker's brand take on a screen that's ultimately about the customer's decision? Each direction answered it differently — from a loud Santander takeover to a calm neutral layout.

A constraint we held from day one

Data is incomplete for some recommendations — external deals don't surface rates or fees upfront. The design had to handle missing values gracefully, relying on advisor context and expectation-setting rather than pretending to know what it doesn't. It's the quiet reason we chose a flexible, neutral header over any of the data-rich branded options.

08 / Impact

What happened after we shipped.

Four weeks post-launch, across 5.4M daily active customers.

0% recommendations accepted in-app (vs. sense-check + leave)
0% drop-off at the Monzo→web handoff
0 days average time from recommendation to decision
0pts "I felt confident making this decision" (post-journey survey)

"Laura reframed the brief. We came in thinking we needed a prettier recommendation screen and left having shipped a decision moment. That reframing is what made the numbers move."

09 / Reflection

What I'd take to the next one.

  • Reframing the brief is half the design. Moving from "discovery problem" to "decision, timing, and trust problem under uncertainty" is what unlocked everything else.
  • Watch people actually deciding. Usability tests show whether a flow works. Decision-moment research shows whether customers will commit. They're different research practices and the second is rarer.
  • The handoff is the product. Every cross-surface seam is a moment where intent leaks. Treat handoffs as first-class design surfaces, not connective tissue.
