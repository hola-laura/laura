01 / The problem02 / The design03 / The learning
01 / Understand the work

Risk reporting should not be another risk.

Nurses, risk managers and quality officers faced repeated screens, unclear navigation and little feedback on what had happened.

I combined interviews, an interface audit and workshops to find the points where work stalled.

02 / Make the important work easier

A home for risks.
A clear next action.

I brought reporting and “My Risks” together, made actions available beside each risk, and separated local navigation from the wider product.

For longer forms, a contents list helped people find their place. We kept the familiar term “risk trackers” after testing alternatives.

From sketch to a testable journey

Work through the flow before polishing the screen.

03 / Know what the test did—and did not—prove

A stronger workflow.
A change of direction.

Most participants completed the test tasks without help. Some still found the section dashboards distracting, which gave us a clear area to simplify.

The project stopped when business priorities changed after a merger. The usability work gave us a stronger design to build on, but we could not measure it in everyday use.

I would carry the clearer workflow forward, then test it in day-to-day use before claiming an operational benefit.
