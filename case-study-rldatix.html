The problem

One platform that still felt like many

Datix Cloud IQ ↗ brought hospital safety tools into one platform, but each screen still behaved differently. During COVID-19, that cost staff time they didn’t have.

Hard to navigate

Mixed navigation levels and inconsistent layouts made it difficult for staff to know where they were or what to do next.

Unclear feedback

Vanishing menus and unclear system responses interrupted tasks and left staff unsure whether an action had worked.

Repeated work

Without shared components, product teams solved the same interface problems separately. We needed patterns they could reuse.

The same few problems repeated everywhere. So one good fix could improve dozens of screens.

02 / HOW IT BEHAVES

Designing how it behaves, not just how it looks

The old screens only had a happy path. Clear feedback was one of the first shared patterns, so with Engineering we agreed what a save does in every state it can end in. It became part of the library, and every team got the same behaviour for free.

03 / TESTING

Test the decisions.
Listen to the people using them.

Every big decision went in front of healthcare professionals before it shipped. Sometimes, like here, they changed it.

A DECISION CHANGED BY RESEARCH

Familiar language mattered more than a simpler label.

  1. 01 / HYPOTHESIS

    Try a clearer name

    Could “Grouped Risks” make “Risk trackers” easier to understand?

  2. 02 / FINDING

    Familiarity was clearer

    Clinicians already understood “Risk trackers”. A new label added confusion.

  3. 03 / DECISION

    Keep the term. Improve the flow.

    I kept “Risk trackers” and improved navigation hierarchy and the visibility of risk actions.

04 / My decisions

What I decided, and what it changed.

  1. 01

    Keep the word clinicians knew.

    Situation
    A simpler label, “Grouped risks”, was on the table for “Risk trackers”.
    Call
    In testing, the new label made people hesitate. I kept the term and fixed the navigation instead.
    Result
    Support tickets about navigation stopped.
  2. 02

    Three admin flows, one journey.

    Situation
    Admins set up icons, labels and destination forms in three separate places.
    Call
    One configuration journey, with a live preview of what staff will see.
    Result
    3 → 1 flows, tested with 4 major clients.
  3. 03

    Speak it, don’t cut it.

    Situation
    Reports took 20–30 minutes, but governance teams needed every field.
    Call
    Keep the fields. Let staff speak a first draft and review it before sending.
    Result
    A first draft in about 5 minutes.
  4. 04

    A shared library, built with Engineering.

    My part
    The components and how they behave, and the research that set priorities.
    Their part
    Engineering built the library and rolled it out across product teams.
    Result
    Support tickets about saving stopped.

What I learned: one product isn’t a fresh coat of paint. It’s components that behave the same everywhere, setup that works for each hospital, and words clinicians already use.
