01 / Reframe the proposition

The experience said “pay later”. Customers wanted a credit card.

Acquisition was 15% behind plan. I partnered with marketing to connect credit-card positioning with a product that could deliver on it.

One proposition, from first impression to everyday use.

02 / Simplify the mental model

One account.
The numbers that matter.

Research with 20 customers showed that people wanted to manage their whole balance, rather than edit purchases one by one.

I brought balance, payments, activity and settings into one modular view.

9/10participants completed tasks unaided in the second usability round.
03 / Give control, with clarity

Show what paying extra actually changes.

I made the remaining balance and interest saving visible, then added deliberate friction when an extra payment would not reduce the next monthly payment.

In a credit product, understanding the consequence matters more than saving a tap.

The next layer of control

Change this month.
See the months ahead.

Monthly-payment editing followed in a later phase. Customers could see how a change affected future payments and interest before committing.

A little more on the decisions & learnings

What I owned

Design across Growth and Core: the audit, product vision, customer journeys, prototypes, testing with our part-time researcher and a shared product map. I worked with two PMs, eight engineers, data, marketing, legal and compliance.

Where I changed the plan

Early testing with eight customers exposed confusion around numbers and structure. I simplified the vision before testing again with ten customers. Nine completed tasks unaided; three still needed clearer explanations of defaults.

What we phased

Engineering could not merge paying a monthly amount with editing future payments without complex allocation changes. I separated the journeys, kept consequences explicit and phased the more complex controls.

What I would improve next

Make credit-card payment cycles easier to understand and audit colour contrast. The launch also reinforced the value of small cohorts and documenting edge cases as they emerged.

Reading the impact

The headline results are the wider launch figures reported in the Figma retrospective. Acquisition framing experiments were separate. The reporting period and definitions behind the launch metrics still need confirmation.
