01 / Reframe the promise

The product said “pay later”. Customers already used credit cards.

Launched in 2021, Flex had reached 350k customers by November 2022, but acquisition was still 15% behind plan. A survey of 1,924 eligible customers showed a mismatch: 65% used credit cards; 9% used BNPL. I used this evidence with Marketing to reframe Flex around a credit-card proposition.

We made the price clear: 0% interest when paying in full or over three months; 24% APR over six or twelve instalments, with no hidden fees and fee-free overseas use.

The framing lifted account creation by 7.1%. In a separate awareness test, awareness rose from 0.65% to 0.77% (20% relative uplift).

02 / Bring the account together

One balance. A clearer starting point.

37% of Flex customers had more than ten active purchases. Managing them one by one made the account hard to scan.

I brought the virtual card, total balance, available limit, next payments and activity into one view, with clear actions to pay early or settle the full balance.

9/10participants completed tasks unaided in the second usability round.
03 / Make repayments understandable

Show what each choice changes.

Customers could pay in full, pay the monthly minimum, or choose per purchase. When an extra payment would not reduce the next scheduled payment, the flow explained that before confirmation.

I made the remaining balance and interest saving visible. The confirmation step added friction where it could prevent a costly misunderstanding.

Phased after the first release

Change this month. See the months ahead.

Changing a monthly amount alongside future payments required complex allocation changes. We shipped the unified account and simpler actions first, then added an edit view showing the impact on upcoming payments and interest.

04 / Make the system shippable

One account view; deeper controls phased in.

With 37% of customers managing more than ten active purchases, V1 needed to show the whole account at a glance. I prioritized the unified balance, next payment, repayment defaults and simple actions.

Allocation rules made monthly-payment editing a broader engineering change, so we phased it. That let us ship the clearer account first and extend repayment controls later.

05 / Lead across teams

One customer promise. Shared decisions across Growth and Core.

The survey gave Marketing evidence for the credit-card framing. Across Growth and Core, I sequenced the work around customer value, business impact and engineering effort; rollout expanded in 50k-customer cohorts.

IN HINDSIGHT

Bring allocation edge cases forward

I would map payment-allocation constraints with Engineering earlier, before detailed flow design.

NEXT HYPOTHESIS

Can customers predict the impact?

After a 50k cohort, I would test whether customers can predict how a repayment change affects the next payment and total interest before expanding the controls.

Research and delivery detail

Project goals

The brief set three targets for the end of 2023: grow to 700k customers, increase average customer spending from £200 to £400, and raise the active-customer rate from 47% to 60%. Flex also needed to move to Monzo’s new app framework.

My role

I led design across Growth and Core, working with two PMs, eight engineers, Data, Marketing, Legal and Compliance, alongside a part-time researcher.

Testing

Early testing with eight customers exposed confusion around numbers and structure. After simplifying the vision, nine of ten participants completed tasks unaided in the second round.

System contribution

The account summary, repayment default and consequence explanation became repeatable patterns in the Flex journeys. The project record does not document a contribution to Monzo’s shared design-system library.

Accessibility follow-up

The project record does not document screen-reader testing or research specifically with financially vulnerable customers; I would validate both, alongside contrast, before extending the controls.

Delivery learnings

Research helped shape the experience and align the teams. Flex was the first product on Monzo’s new app framework, so sharing work early with Core helped make the patterns reusable.

Ongoing collaboration with Risk, Legal and Compliance helped resolve edge cases and ship faster. Releasing in 50k-customer cohorts reduced launch risk; bugs found after implementation were documented and added to the roadmap.
