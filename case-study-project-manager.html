01 / Overview

My team identified a strategic opportunity: positioning Monzo as the "Project Manager" for first-time home buyers. The team had strategy, research, and data — but no plan for how to actually test it. I was brought in to turn all of that into a concrete experiment.

Rather than jumping into screens, I spent my first days reading everything — strategy docs, workshop outputs, data science analysis, and pilot scoping documents. I connected dots that existed in silos and came to the team with a clear recommendation: test targeting and positioning sequentially, not simultaneously.

03 / The problem

The team had strategy but no experiment plan

A cross-functional workshop had identified the core insight: first-time buyers don't need more services — they need someone to help them understand, coordinate, and feel in control of the whole process.

The pilot doc had three positioning routes drafted, but critical gaps:

  • The "What we want to learn" table was empty
  • No success criteria defined
  • Surfaces not confirmed (email? home feed? bento?)
  • Targeting approach still open
  • No clarity on whether the three routes differ in visual treatment or just copy

Most critically: nobody had addressed whether targeting and positioning should be tested together or separately. This is the question that shapes the entire experiment.

04 / My approach

Read the data, not just the briefs

I started by connecting three documents that existed in silos:

  • The strategy workshop told me why — customer pain themes and the Project Manager vision
  • The data science analysis told me who — how to find FTBs with an ML model
  • The pilot scoping doc told me what — three positioning routes to test

The missing piece was how. That's where I focused.

The critical design decision

If you change both who sees it (targeting) and what they see (positioning) at the same time, you can't attribute results to either variable. A better result could come from reaching the right people or from saying the right thing — and you'd never know which.

My recommendation: test sequentially.

05 / Positioning routes

Three ways to frame the same proposition

Route A · Baseline

Your mortgage journey, all in Monzo.

Fee-free expert mortgage advice, brought together in one simple experience.

Emotional territory: Confidence and ease
Bad guy: Mortgage journeys spread across different apps, emails, and providers
Route B · Paid PM

Stop managing your home move on your own.

Mortgage, legal work, and survey coordinated into one experience — less chasing, more moving forward.

Emotional territory: Relief, reassurance, feeling looked after
Bad guy: A stressful, fragmented process where buyers coordinate everything themselves
Route C · Free PM

Your home-buying journey, made clearer in Monzo.

Fee-free mortgage advice plus guidance and visibility that helps you feel in control.

Emotional territory: Clarity, momentum, and control
Bad guy: Feeling overwhelmed and constantly unsure what's going on

08 / Success criteria

I proposed the framework the pilot doc left blank

The scoping document had an empty success criteria table. Rather than waiting for someone else to fill it, I proposed a three-tier framework that gives the team clear decision points.

We have confidence when…

  • Targeted users click at 2×+ rate vs untargeted
  • At least one PM route outperforms baseline on CTR
  • Qualitative feedback shows FTBs understand and value the concept
  • Referral-to-application conversion exceeds current baseline
  • Users describe it as "helpful" not "salesy"

We need more data when…

  • Clicks are up but conversion is flat vs untargeted
  • PM routes perform similarly to baseline — territory resonates but framing doesn't differentiate
  • Sample size too small for statistical significance
  • Users engage but drop off at a specific funnel point
  • Different surfaces show conflicting results

We don't have confidence when…

  • No meaningful CTR difference between targeted and untargeted
  • PM positioning performs worse than baseline
  • Qualitative feedback shows confusion or indifference
  • Users see the content but don't engage at all
  • High click-through but zero downstream conversion
09 / Reflection

What makes this lead-level work

DecisionWhy
Sequential testing (targeting first)Can't isolate variables if you change both audience and message simultaneously
Bento as primary surfaceDesigned for personalised content, launching imminently, cleanest test environment
Baseline as Phase 1 messageNeed a stable, neutral proposition to validate the targeting model
Sketchy wireframes over polished UIEarly-stage alignment tool — visual polish isn't justified until experiment structure is agreed
Three-tier success criteriaClear decision points rather than binary pass/fail that could kill a promising direction prematurely

The thing I'm most proud of in this work isn't a screen — it's the recommendation. "Test targeting and positioning sequentially" is a design decision that shapes every artifact that follows. A lead designer doesn't just make things look good; they figure out what to make and in what order.

I also proactively filled gaps nobody asked me to fill. The success criteria framework, the data callouts connecting the ML model to the experiment design, the experiment flow diagram — these were all contributions I brought, not tasks I was assigned.
